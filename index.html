Open Discussion · For Jim

Should we widen the funnel — or hold the qualification line?

Two directions to weigh against each other:

Wider Funnel

Shorten the low-intent survey further, or add a third ultra-short path. Upside: more raw lead volume, lower CPL, faster Meta optimization. Risk: qualification work shifts to the sales team, which is already capacity-constrained. CAC stays low on paper but rises in human hours.

Deeper Qualification

Hold the current two-tier structure (or tighten it further). Upside: every lead arrives with intent data, the team works the right list first, conversion-to-consult ratio climbs. Risk: fewer raw leads, slower learning signal for Meta, higher reported CPL even if true ROI is better.

The Real Question

Are we optimizing for cost per lead — or cost per qualified consult? The two answers send us down very different roads.

Open Discussion · For Jim

How aggressive should our automation cadence be?

A 30-day drip is the industry baseline — but for a $25K–$50K decision, more touches risk fatigue while fewer touches risk the lead going cold. Two ways to read this:

More Touches

Higher message frequency keeps us top-of-mind for leads with longer decision cycles. Upside: better recall when the patient is finally ready. Risk: automation fatigue, unsubscribes, brand signal of desperation rather than authority.

Fewer, Sharper Touches

Lower volume, higher signal — each message has to earn its place. Upside: protects brand premium positioning, fewer unsubscribes, more trust. Risk: long-cycle leads forget us, competitors with louder cadence win the recall battle.

The Underlying Question

If our purple cow is warmth and continuity — does aggressive automation contradict the brand we're trying to build? Or is it the only way to stay present long enough to convert?

Open Question · Email Warmup

How should we accelerate sender reputation?

Current approach: daily test sends to internal addresses to build sending pattern. It works, but slowly. Worth exploring with the team:

  • Warmup tools. Services like Mailwarm, Warmup Inbox, or Lemwarm automate engagement signals (opens, replies, clicks) at scale.
  • Gradual volume ramp. Start at 20–50 sends/day, increase ~20% weekly. Avoid sudden spikes that trigger spam filters.
  • Engagement-first content. The first 30 days should prioritize emails that get replies — not just opens. Replies are the strongest deliverability signal.
  • Authentication audit. Verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records are correctly configured on the sending domain (not just the GHL subdomain).
  • List hygiene. Suppress unengaged contacts after 60–90 days to keep open rates high — engagement metrics are what mailbox providers actually score.

Worth getting Jim's perspective on what worked at scale at ClearChoice. Email is still our highest-leverage nurture channel, but only if it lands in the inbox.

07 · Honest Diagnostic

Where the Funnel Breaks

This is the section we most want your eyes on. We have a working system. We do not yet have a system that consistently produces the right patient at the right cost.

The Core Insight

The biggest problem is not lead volume — it is qualified leads.

  • AffordabilityMany leads cannot finance treatment at the price point required.
  • ReadinessSome are educated and intrigued, but not yet ready to commit.
  • Friction Drop-OffOthers fall out of the funnel due to fear, confusion, or process friction.

Discussion · For the Meeting

Where should we plant our flag — and where can we credibly defend it?

Three positions worth pressure-testing:

  • Top-Left · Premium Value. Specialist-level care without the corporate markup. Anti-ClearChoice positioning. Risk: requires either a real cost advantage (we don't have one) or a hero who is willing to charge less than the market.
  • Top-Right · Concierge. The Nuvia move. Charge premium, deliver something nobody else can — your surgeon's personal cell, the 35-year promise, "All on Everyday." Risk: requires the operational backbone to defend the price.
  • Top-Center · Trusted Specialist. Slightly above-mid price, clearly above-mid quality. Lean on credentials, continuity, and surgeon-led care. Lowest-risk position. Highest-risk in five years if the market polarizes.
