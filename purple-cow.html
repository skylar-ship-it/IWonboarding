Finding Our Purple Cow.

If a patient Googled every full-arch surgeon in the Midwest tonight — could they tell us apart? This is the workshop where we find the answer. Built on Seth Godin's framework and 150 PickFu respondents.

01 · The Framework

Two Kinds of Cow.

Seth Godin's idea: in a field of brown cows, only a purple one is remarkable. Most full-arch practices say the same six things — specialists, in-house lab, zirconia, same-day teeth, transparent pricing, lifetime warranty. If everyone says it, it isn't different.

B

The Brown Cow

Better. Faster. Cheaper. These are improvements — not differentiation. Every claim that competitors can also make is a brown cow, no matter how true it is for you.

What Brown Cows Sound Like "Same-day teeth." "Specialists on staff." "Lifetime warranty." "Transparent pricing."
P

The Purple Cow

Different. Testable, defensible, owned. A claim that's specific enough to be falsifiable, structurally protected by your operations, and not something every competitor can credibly say.

What Purple Cows Sound Like Nuvia: "Permanent teeth in 24 hours." New Life: "$9,990 per arch. Why pay $40,000?"

The only word that matters is different.

03 · Synthesis

Three Truths That Survived.

Across both polls, three signals emerged consistently — each one a candidate raw material for our purple cow. The challenge: most competitors could borrow any of them. Our job is to make them ownable.

i.

Continuity Wins

"One surgeon, no handoffs" was the highest-conviction theme across all 150 respondents. Patients are afraid of being passed between specialists. They want a single, accountable human from consult to year-five follow-up.

ii.

Warmth Beats Status

Family treatment, long-term relationship, "feels like home" out-converted championship-level credentials 60/40. Authority belongs as proof, not as the lead message. Soften credentials — don't lean on them.

iii.

Tech Repels — Unless Surgeon-Led

AI as a hero message lost 87/13. Patients want a person, not a system. Technology is welcome only when framed as a tool the surgeon uses — never as a co-author of their care.

The opportunity: take "one surgeon, no handoffs" — and make it uncopyable.
